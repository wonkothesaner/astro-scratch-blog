I stopped paid employment on 25th May 2026.

I had started with Jaguar Motor Cars in 1988, the year I left school.

In the years between 1998 and 2026, I was paid by Jaguar, Quails Catering, A.W.Chapman, Marshall Specialist Vehicles, Druid (with secondment to Synergy) that was bought by FI Group, which then became Xansa, Capita, and Capgemini.

Apart from waiting on tables and cleaning dishes in a golf club, I worked in two different industry sectors - Automotive manufacturing and IT, specifically implementnig SAP.

Along the way, I collected any number of memories - some fond, others less so.

- In 1988 as part of Jaguar's training program, I was taught it's impossible to file a piece of metal perfectly flat
<Image src={mondeoImage} alt="Image of 1990s Ford Mondeo" class="float-right" widths={[200, 400, 600]} sizes="33vw" />
- At the end of 1992, I walked around a Ford Motor Company car park in Genk counting how many Mondeos were missing their driver or passenger seats. I worked for the company that supplied the seat runners and, as I came to understand was normal, we had not tooled up to support full production volumes. Unfortunately, it turned out that the Mondeo was not normal and was the first car in living memory that hit full production volumes.  Consequently, over a period of weeks, we slowly ran out of parts to supply them. Once our problem became clear with seat-less cars starting to be parked in the car park, the Ford Genk site Director paid our manufacturing facility a visit. He couldn't quite believe that I, a 23-year-old, was in charge of our (admittedly small) Just-In-Time (JIT) production line and left in a huff having told us to "pull our finger out".
- Shop Stewards (in my case representing members in both a Paint and Metal Fabrication shop) are just as likely to dislike talking to 25-year-old manager as a Ford site Director.
- Attending an interview for a job I knew nothing about (the newspaper advert literally said "Don't worry if you don't know what SAP is") and finding that my two interviewers were late because they had been playing squash at lunchtime.  I got the job. 
- Travelling business class (them sure were the good ole days!) to Philadelphia in the mid-1990s to work my first day in the States.  It was a day-flight, I travelled in a suit (I was a consultant after all) and got laughed out of SAP's Newton Square offices as soon as I arrived.  I wore polo-shirts and chinos from that point forward.
- Spending 2-3 years each living in Boston (with skiing in Vermont), San Diego, and Singapore - all part of a "travel the world" implementing SAP lifestyle.  Travelling to many connected locations, e.g. Hawaii, Chicago, Seatlle, New Orleans, Montreal, Cambodia, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Mexico in between.  
- Living and working in the middle of London - running around Battersea Park in the early morning, walking to and from work along the river, going out in the evening. 
- Bringing up two wonderful children before leaving London for many reasons but mainly Brexit, schooling, and space.
